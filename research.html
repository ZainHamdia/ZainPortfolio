Abstract: Analyzed Princeton's legacy ~20-year-old MAE 412 DCC switch thrower schematic and redesigned it into a modern, single-sided PCB. 1. Background & Legacy Analysis

Part of Princeton University’s engineering curriculum is a class centered on microprocessors and their applications (MAE 412). This class uses model train sets as the platform for students to create and test their own automated model train sets. One of the most useful tools is a DCC railroad track switch thrower with a trickle-charged capacitor. With the original board’s design being ~20 years old and the limits becoming clear, it was decided to modernize the board.

Analysis of the legacy schematic (Figure 1) showed it drew power from the track or an optional 5V input, charging a capacitor over ~12 seconds before discharging through a relay to throw the track switch.

2. Modernized Single-Sided PCB Design

The new board introduced two key additions: an onboard ATmega328P chip, eliminating external connections for direct automation, and a second 4700µF capacitor to store additional charge for two rapid throws. The board was engineered as a single-sided PCB (~2 weeks design, ~1 week assembly and hand-soldering), reviewed, approved, and deployed in the lab.
