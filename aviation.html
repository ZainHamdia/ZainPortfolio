SUMMER 2025 • PRINCETON UNIVERSITY MAE

Joseph Henry Project Research Internship

Department of Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering

During the summer of 2025, Zain served as a high school research intern for the Joseph Henry Project in the Department of Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering (MAE) at Princeton University, working under the guidance of Professor Michael Littman.

The Joseph Henry Project focuses on the physical, historical, and scientific reconstruction of pivotal laboratory apparatus invented by the 19th-century American physicist Joseph Henry (1799–1878). Henry's work laid foundational groundwork for mutual inductance, electromagnetism, and the earliest electromagnetic motors and acoustic telegraphic relays.

Connecting Historical Physics to Modern Aerospace: Reconstructing historic electromechanical apparatus requires dissecting the fundamental relationship between electric current, magnetic flux, and mechanical force. Understanding these first-principle mechanisms directly informs modern aerospace applications—such as flight control solenoids, electric actuators, and sensor instrumentation.

Through this laboratory research, Zain gained firsthand experience with physical scientific replication: winding electromagnetic coils, analyzing magnetic field behavior, calibrating mechanical linkages, and documenting experimental findings with academic precision.

ONGOING EXPLORATION • AERODYNAMICS

Aeronautical & Flight Dynamics Studies

Aerospace Engineering Preparation

With the goal of studying aerospace engineering at the university level, Zain pursues independent studies and projects centered on aerodynamics and flight principles.

This work includes analyzing airfoil cross-sections, studying the trade-offs between lift and drag across varying angles of attack, and exploring aircraft stability margins (center of gravity relative to center of pressure). To ground theoretical equations in practical experience, Zain builds and experiments with radio-controlled model aircraft, observing how control surface deflections and weight distribution impact flight stability and glide performance.

By combining theoretical physics from the classroom with hands-on flight models, he is actively building the technical foundation required for advanced aerospace studies.
