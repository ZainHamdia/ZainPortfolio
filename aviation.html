PRINCETON UNIVERSITY • DEPT. OF MECHANICAL & AEROSPACE ENGINEERING

Joseph Henry Project Research Internship

Faculty Advisor: Professor Michael Littman • Summer 2025
Research Abstract: The Joseph Henry Project investigates the physical, mechanical, and electromagnetic apparatus created by 19th-century American scientist Joseph Henry (1799–1878). As a high school research intern, Zain collaborated on the precise historical reconstruction of Henry's seminal experimental devices, exploring the foundational mechanics of mutual inductance, electromagnetic coil design, and early electric motors.

Reconstructing historical scientific instruments requires analyzing how electromagnetic force can be mechanically harnessed to generate reliable physical motion. Working under Professor Littman, Zain investigated:

  • Electromagnetic Induction: How wire gauge, winding density, and core permeability influence magnetic flux and attractive force.
  • Apparatus Replication: Translating 19th-century scientific records and mechanical descriptions into functioning, physically calibrated laboratory models.
  • First-Principle Physics: Studying the exact principles that directly underpin modern aerospace electric motors, actuators, and flight instrumentation.

This collegiate research experience deepened Zain's understanding of experimental rigor, sensor calibration, and empirical validation in a formal university laboratory setting.

AERODYNAMIC EXPLORATION • FLIGHT MECHANICS

Aeronautical & Flight Dynamics Studies

Aerospace Engineering Preparation

Driven by a long-term goal of studying aerospace engineering at the undergraduate level, Zain conducts independent study and practical experiments into flight physics and aerodynamics.

Key Areas of Investigation: • Airfoil Aerodynamics: Analyzing camber, thickness distribution, and lift/drag polars.
• Aircraft Stability: Understanding static margin, dynamic stability, and the center-of-gravity (CG) envelope.
• Model Flight Testing: Evaluating radio-controlled aircraft control surfaces, elevator trim, and thrust-to-weight performance.

By testing flight characteristics on radio-controlled models, Zain connects mathematical equations of lift and drag to observable physical behavior in the air.
