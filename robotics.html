Zainiac Robot Series

Mechanical Chassis Design, Microcontrollers, Sensory Integration & Prototyping

The Zainiac Robot Series represents an ongoing lineage of original mobile robots conceived, designed, and constructed independently by Zain Hamdia. Starting with his landmark first build, Zainiac 19, the series incorporates mechanical design, electronics, self-taught Arduino programming, fabrication, and iterative prototyping.

🚀 Zainiac 19
First independent build. Self-taught Arduino programming, motor driver circuits, chassis assembly & ultrasonic obstacle sensing. Debuted at Philadelphia & Brussels.
⚙️ Zainiac 21
Second-generation architecture. Enhanced mechanical packaging, refined sensor response, and modular electronics bay.
⚡ Zainiac 25
Current iteration. Advanced mobile platform integrating modern CAD iteration, optimized weight distribution, and durable drive kinematics.
// CORE ENGINEERING DISCIPLINES:
• Mechanical Design: Custom chassis layouts, drivetrain assembly, and structural framing.
• Electronics & Control: Self-taught Arduino programming, sensor integration, and motor driver wiring.
• Iterative Prototyping: Evolution through successive generations (Zainiac 19 → Zainiac 21 → Zainiac 25).

Little Bot & Noggin

Kinematics Exploration, Locomotion Mechanics & Compact Control Packaging

Independent robotic projects developed to explore specific mechanics of robotic locomotion, hardware packaging, and motor controls.

These projects provided focused testbeds for experimenting with compact chassis geometry, degrees of freedom, and testing responsive control inputs.

// KINEMATICS & CONTROL STUDY
Focuses on actuator placement, center of mass balancing, degrees of freedom, and testing micro-chassis mechanical stability.

// COMMUNITY HEALTHCARE FABRICATION

3D-Printed N95 Masks (COVID-19 Relief)

Manufactured for & Donated to New York Cancer Center

During the height of personal protective equipment shortages during the COVID-19 pandemic, Zain designed and produced 3D-printed N95 masks, donating them directly to a New York cancer center to protect vulnerable patients and medical staff.

// HEALTHCARE IMPACT & PUBLISHED RESEARCH
Donated directly to front-line oncology wards during critical supply-chain disruption. This humanitarian fabrication project directly inspired his published article: “From COVID-19 to Zainiac 19”.
